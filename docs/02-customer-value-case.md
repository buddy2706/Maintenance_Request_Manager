# Customer Value Case — Recognized Value, Not Sentiment

*Framed for a CEO audience.*

**The value customers cannot do without.** Property management companies don't buy maintenance software to log tickets — they buy it to make maintenance *stop consuming their people*. The recognized value is **coordination labor that disappears**: every work order that resolves without a manager acting as switchboard is time returned to higher-value work (leasing, owner relations, growth). Once a customer experiences their team spending less time relaying messages, they cannot go back to doing it manually. That is the value they will not give up.

**The risk of not solving it.** If we don't own the coordination layer, we remain a system of record for a process that happens *around* us — in texts, calls, and inboxes we can't see. A system of record that doesn't reduce work is a line item to be cut. Competitors positioning on "we run your maintenance for you" will define the category, and we'll be reduced to a database they integrate with or replace.

**Business viability impact.**
- **Retention:** Maintenance is the most frequent touchpoint; a product that visibly reduces its pain is the product a customer renews to keep. Coordination relief is a stickiness engine.
- **Expansion:** Value that scales with volume (labor saved per work order) means larger portfolios derive more value — the natural path to seat/portfolio expansion and premium tiers.
- **Renewal defensibility:** When the workflow is where their maintenance *runs*, ripping us out means re-absorbing the labor. Switching cost becomes operational, not just contractual.

**Evidence supporting the value case.**
- Coordination, not repair, is the dominant time cost in maintenance. [Cycle-time data]
- Managers self-describe as message-relayers — a role they resent and would pay to eliminate. [Interview evidence]
- Status-chasing is a large share of resident contact volume — pure overhead we can erase. [Support data]
- `[Named customers]` already improvise workarounds (shared inboxes, spreadsheets, group texts) to close this gap — demand is proven; they're just solving it badly without us.

**The ask.** Fund the coordination layer as a value driver, not a feature. It's the difference between being the software maintenance is *recorded in* and the software maintenance *runs on* — and only the second one renews and expands.
